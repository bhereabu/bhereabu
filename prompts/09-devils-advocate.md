# 9. Devil's advocate

You've made up your mind. You've already thought through the objections. You're
convinced. That's exactly the moment to have the decision attacked — not polite
concerns, the full case against.

Five minutes now, before you commit. Not after.

## Paste at the start of a new chat

```
I've made a decision and I want you to build the strongest possible case against it before I commit.

The decision: [describe exactly what you're planning to do]
My reasoning: [why you think it's a good idea]
What I've already considered: [objections you've already thought about]

Your job:
- Build the strongest possible case AGAINST this decision.
- Don't balance it with positives. I already believe in it - I need the counterargument.
- Find the assumptions I'm making that could be wrong.
- Describe the 3 most realistic ways this fails or backfires.
- Tell me what I'm probably underestimating.
- Tell me what I would need to believe for this to be a genuinely bad idea.

Be ruthless. If this is a mistake, I need to know now.
```

## Why the third field matters

"What I've already considered" is the field people skip, and it's the one that
makes the output useful. Without it you get the three objections you thought of
last week. With it, Claude has to go past them.

## What to do with the output

Not everything it raises is real. Sort the objections into three piles:

- **Fatal if true** — go verify it before committing. This is the whole point.
- **Costly but survivable** — decide now what you'd do, so it isn't a surprise.
- **Noise** — write one sentence on why it doesn't apply, then drop it.

The last prompt line is the sharpest one. "What would I need to believe for this
to be a bad idea" converts a vague unease into a testable claim, and a testable
claim can be checked before you spend the money.
